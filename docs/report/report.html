LAST MAGAZINE

Assignment 2 report · Taha Keler · Godot 4.7.2 (GDScript) · original 3D game (not a tutorial extension)

1. Game brief

LAST MAGAZINE is a first person survival horror shooter that I made from scratch in Godot 4.7. You are stuck inside a dark stone labyrinth with a pistol, a shotgun and a rifle, and the zombies just keep coming at you. The main idea is ammo. You never really have enough of it, and every kill only gives a little bit back, so you have to keep fighting if you want to keep shooting. You also get a torch, a melee shove and a decoy you can throw, and there are supply caches and medkits hidden around the map. I spent a lot of time on the atmosphere too: there is cave reverb, sounds get muffled behind walls, you sometimes hear fake footsteps behind you when it's quiet, and your heartbeat kicks in when your health is low.

Goal and result. In the default mode, Extraction, you have to stay alive until a 2 minute extraction clock runs out, and every kill takes some time off the clock. Win: the clock hits zero and you are still alive ("EXTRACTED"). Loss: your health hits zero ("YOU DIED"). There are three other modes that use the same loop: Last Stand (survive 5 minutes), Endless (your score is how long you last) and Signal (arm three relays and then get back to where you started).

Progress display. The HUD shows the extraction countdown, kills, how many zombies are alive, your health (a number and a segmented bar), magazine and reserve ammo, a compass strip and a minimap. Restart: you can press Play again on the results screen, or hit Enter any time. Both call Game.start_round(), which resets the player (position, health, stance), the weapons (magazines and reserves), the zombies (all removed, spawner reset), the pickups, objectives, timers and counters.

Interaction and collision. The mouse and keyboard control a CharacterBody3D in 3D, and a few different collisions do something in the game. When the gun's raycast hits a zombie's collision capsule it does damage, and the zombie dies at zero. When a zombie's lunge reaches the player's body, the player takes damage. And each ammo cache has an Area3D that notices when the player walks into it (body_entered), which is what lets you take ammo from it.

3. The two features

Feature 1: Ammunition and reload

TriggerLeft click (fire) and R (reload). If you try to shoot with an empty magazine it also starts a reload on its own.
State change and ownerThe Weapon node (Player/Head/Camera/Weapon, script src/gameplay/weapon/weapon.gd) owns magazine_ammo and reserve_ammo. try_fire() takes away one round. If the magazine is empty the shot is blocked and it emits dry_fired. try_reload() starts a timed reload (1.5 s for the pistol) that moves rounds from the reserve into the magazine. It won't start if the magazine is already full or the reserve is empty. Kills give a bit of reserve ammo back.
Visible resultThe HUD (src/ui/hud.gd: _on_ammo_changed, _on_reload_started, _on_dry_fired) updates the number, turns it red at 0 and shows RELOADING. The gun also dips down and rolls while it reloads, and you hear a click when you pull the trigger on an empty gun.
Scenes / scriptsweapon.gd, weapon.tscn, weapon_types.gd, hud.gd, hud.tscn, game.gd (ammo from kills).
Built through MCP?No. The AI assistant (Claude Code) wrote this one with its normal file and terminal tools, not through MCP.

Feature 2: Headshots (built through the godot-ai MCP)

TriggerA bullet from the gun's raycast hits a zombie in the head zone, which is the top 16% of its body height (Zombie.is_head_hit()). On the 2.6 m Brute that means anything above 2.18 m.
State change and ownerThe Weapon node doubles the damage for that bullet (headshot_multiplier = 2) and emits headshot. The Game node (src/core/game.gd) owns the headshots counter. It goes up by 1 for every headshot while the round is running and goes back to 0 when you restart.
Visible resultA red HEADSHOT label flashes above the crosshair and fades out (HeadshotLabel in hud.tscn, shown by HUD.show_headshot()). The zombie goes down noticeably faster, and the results screen shows how many HEADSHOTS you got.
Scenes / scriptsweapon.gd, zombie.gd, game.gd, hud.gd, hud.tscn.
Built through MCP?Yes. I connected Claude Code to my open Godot editor with the godot-ai MCP server, and every change for this feature was done with MCP tool calls. It used script_patch on all four scripts. For the scene it used scene_open, node_create to add the new HeadshotLabel, 17 node_set_property calls (text, anchors, offsets, colours, outline, mouse filter, hidden at the start) and scene_save. The MCP also caught a mistake by itself: the first patch to zombie.gd used head_fraction before it was declared, and the next patch fixed it.

Also: player health (how you lose)

Health is what ends the round. Trigger: a zombie's lunge reaches the player (Zombie._try_land_hit). State owner: the Health node under Player (src/gameplay/health.gd). Visible result: the number and bar go down, a red arc points at whoever hit you, the edges of the screen pulse red under 35%, and at 0 you get YOU DIED. I tested this the same way. A Shambler's hit takes you from 100 to 88 (normal). Getting hit for 12 at 5 health leaves you at exactly 0 (boundary), not a negative number, with one death, the round lost, and a second hit that gets ignored. After restarting you're back to 100 health, 0 kills and 10 / 24 ammo.

4. Corrective prompt log: mouse look didn't work

What I wantedMoving the mouse or my MacBook trackpad turns the camera. From the start I said camera look should only be mouse or trackpad, never the arrow keys.
What I gotWhen I played, the camera didn't move with the mouse at all, even though every automated test passed.

Original prompt (my requirement from the early sessions): camera look has to use the mouse or trackpad, never the arrow keys, and it has to work on a MacBook trackpad. Response: the assistant made the Player a CharacterBody3D with mouse look in _unhandled_input that captures the mouse when you click (commit "Add first-person player controller"). Later on it also added a HUD with a crosshair made of five small pieces.

Follow-up prompt 1 (mine, copied exactly):

"for the mini map can you jsut get the 2d view of the original map and do it like that, also the mouse doesnt work, fix that immidiatelly and continue on what you where doing"

Response 1 (the assistant, copied exactly): "Found it. ColorRect defaults to mouse_filter = STOP, and the crosshair's five rects sit dead centre — which is exactly where a captured cursor is pinned. They swallow every InputEventMouseMotion before _unhandled_input ever runs. Every headless test passes because none of them has a cursor sitting over a Control." It was also honest that it couldn't reproduce the bug in its own tests, so "the final word is yours at the keyboard."

Cause. In Godot, any Control under the mouse gets the mouse events first, and _unhandled_input only gets whatever nothing else used. The crosshair pieces were ColorRects left on the default MOUSE_FILTER_STOP, and they were sitting exactly where the captured mouse is, so the player never got any mouse movement at all. The tests didn't catch it because headless Godot never has a mouse over a Control.

Fix. Every crosshair piece in src/ui/hud.tscn got set to mouse_filter = 2 (IGNORE). We also added a test for it (tests/manual/verify_look.gd, test_look_nothing_on_the_hud_swallows_the_mouse) that sends real mouse movement from where the cursor is and fails if anything on the HUD blocks it. After that I kept the same rule for every new HUD piece, including the HUD redesign and the HEADSHOT label that the MCP made.

Follow-up prompt 2, the proof (mine, copied exactly, after I played it on my MacBook). The test for it also passes in the project's check script (tools/check.sh, exit 0), and there's a second correction in the appendix.

"Its good, the mouse finally works, tho there is a problem, i would prefer that the zombies always run at me …"

Appendix B: a second correction, zombies standing still

My prompt (copied exactly): "i would prefer that the zombies always run at me, instead of just staying there in there places"

First result (wrong): the assistant made every zombie keep "smelling" where I am so it always walks towards me. Then it measured a real round (Endless, 20 zombies, me standing still) and it hadn't actually worked. None of the zombies were unaware anymore, but 13 out of 20 were still standing still while "investigating" 15 to 23 m away, and the average distance stayed at 15.0 m after a whole minute.

Cause: the navigation mesh on my labyrinth sits up to 0.7 m above the floor in some places, and Godot's NavigationAgent3D only counts a waypoint as reached within path_desired_distance = 0.6 m, measured in 3D. So the first waypoint was basically the zombie's own spot lifted 0.7 m up. It never got "reached", and the zombie just kept aiming at a point right above its head without moving.

Fix: Zombie._next_waypoint() in src/gameplay/zombie/zombie.gd now measures waypoints flat on the ground and skips to the first point that's actually ahead. Proof: the same test afterwards had all 20 zombies within 6 m by 50 seconds, with an average distance of 1.4 m.
