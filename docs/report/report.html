LAST MAGAZINE

Assignment 2 report · Taha Keler · Godot 4.7.2 (GDScript) · an original 3D game, not a tutorial extension

1. Game brief

LAST MAGAZINE is a first-person survival horror shooter I built from scratch in Godot 4.7. You're stuck in a dark stone labyrinth with a pistol, a shotgun and a rifle, and zombies keep coming for you. The catch is ammunition: there's never enough, and every kill gives back only a little, so you have to keep fighting to keep shooting. You also have a torch, a melee shove and a decoy you can throw, and there are supply caches and medkits hidden around the map. I pushed the atmosphere hard: cave reverb, muffled sounds behind walls, fake footsteps behind you when it's quiet, and a heartbeat when you're low on health.

Goal and result. In the default Extraction mode you have to survive until a 2-minute extraction clock hits zero, and every kill shortens the clock. Win: the clock reaches zero while you're alive ("EXTRACTED"). Loss: your health hits zero ("YOU DIED"). There are three more modes on the same loop: Last Stand (survive 5 minutes), Endless (score = how long you last) and Signal (arm three relays, then get back to the start).

Progress display. The HUD shows the extraction countdown, kills, hostiles alive, health (number and segmented bar), magazine and reserve ammo, a compass strip and a minimap. Restart: Play again on the results screen, or Enter at any time, calls Game.start_round(). That resets the player (position, health, stance), the weapons (magazines and reserves), the zombies (all removed and the spawner reset), pickups, objectives, timers and counters.

Interaction and collision. The mouse and keyboard drive a CharacterBody3D in 3D, and several collisions trigger gameplay. The weapon's raycast hitting a zombie's collision capsule deals damage and kills it at zero. A zombie's lunge reaching the player's body deals contact damage. An ammo cache's Area3D detects the player overlapping it (body_entered), and that's what lets you resupply from it.

3. The two features

Feature 1 — Ammunition and reload

TriggerLeft click (fire) and R (reload). Firing an empty magazine also starts a reload automatically.
State change and ownerThe Weapon node (Player/Head/Camera/Weapon, src/gameplay/weapon/weapon.gd) owns magazine_ammo and reserve_ammo. try_fire() takes one round; an empty magazine blocks the shot and emits dry_fired. try_reload() starts a timed reload (1.5 s on the pistol) that moves rounds from reserve into the magazine. It won't start with a full magazine or an empty reserve. Kills refund a bit of reserve ammo.
Visible resultThe HUD (src/ui/hud.gd: _on_ammo_changed, _on_reload_started, _on_dry_fired) updates the count, turns it red at 0 and shows RELOADING. The gun dips and rolls while reloading, and an empty trigger clicks.
Scenes / scriptsweapon.gd, weapon.tscn, weapon_types.gd; hud.gd, hud.tscn; game.gd (kill refunds).
Built through MCP?No. The AI assistant (Claude Code) wrote it with its own file and shell tools. The file list is in docs/ai-contribution.md.

Feature 2 — Headshots (built through the godot-ai MCP)

TriggerA round from the weapon's raycast hits a zombie in the head zone, the top 16% of its body height (Zombie.is_head_hit()). On a 2.6 m Brute that's anything above 2.18 m.
State change and ownerThe Weapon node doubles that round's damage (headshot_multiplier = 2) and emits headshot. The Game node (src/core/game.gd) owns the headshots counter: +1 per headshot while the round is live, reset to 0 on restart.
Visible resultA red HEADSHOT callout flashes above the crosshair and fades out (HeadshotLabel in hud.tscn, driven by HUD.show_headshot()). The zombie visibly drops faster, and the results screen shows a HEADSHOTS count.
Scenes / scriptsweapon.gd, zombie.gd, game.gd, hud.gd, hud.tscn.
Built through MCP?Yes. I connected Claude Code to the running Godot editor with the godot-ai MCP server, and every change was made with MCP tool calls. script_patch was used on all four scripts. In the scene, scene_open, node_create (the new HeadshotLabel), 17 node_set_property calls (text, anchors, offsets, colours, outline, mouse filter, hidden by default) and scene_save built the callout. The MCP's own diagnostics caught one mistake as it happened: the first zombie.gd patch used head_fraction before it was declared, and the next patch fixed it.

Supporting mechanic — Player health (the loss condition)

Health is what ends the round. Trigger: a zombie's lunge reaches the player (Zombie._try_land_hit). State owner: the Health node under Player (src/gameplay/health.gd). Visible result: the number and bar drop, a red arc points at whoever hit you, the edges of the screen pulse red below 35%, and at 0 you get YOU DIED. I tested it the same way: a Shambler's hit takes you from 100 to 88 (normal). Taking 12 damage at 5 health leaves you at exactly 0 (boundary): not negative, one death, round lost, and a second hit is ignored. After restart it's back to 100 health, 0 kills and 10 / 24 ammo.

4. Corrective prompt log — mouse look didn't work

What I wantedMoving the mouse or my MacBook trackpad turns the first-person camera. My rule from the start: camera look is mouse or trackpad only, never arrow keys.
What I gotIn play the camera didn't respond to the mouse at all, even though every automated test passed.

Original prompt (my requirement from the early sessions): camera look must use the mouse or trackpad, never the arrow keys, and it has to work on a MacBook trackpad. Response: the assistant built the Player as a CharacterBody3D with clamped mouse look in _unhandled_input, capturing the mouse on click (commit "Add first-person player controller"). Later it added a HUD with a five-piece crosshair.

Follow-up prompt 1 (mine, word for word):

"for the mini map can you jsut get the 2d view of the original map and do it like that, also the mouse doesnt work, fix that immidiatelly and continue on what you where doing"

Response 1 (the assistant, word for word): "Found it. ColorRect defaults to mouse_filter = STOP, and the crosshair's five rects sit dead centre — which is exactly where a captured cursor is pinned. They swallow every InputEventMouseMotion before _unhandled_input ever runs. Every headless test passes because none of them has a cursor sitting over a Control." It also told me straight that it couldn't reproduce the bug headlessly, so "the final word is yours at the keyboard."

Cause. In Godot, a Control under the cursor eats mouse events before gameplay code sees them, and _unhandled_input only gets what nobody else consumed. The crosshair pieces were ColorRects left on the default MOUSE_FILTER_STOP, sitting exactly where the captured cursor lives, so the player never got a single mouse-motion event. The tests missed it because headless Godot never hovers a Control.

Fix. Every crosshair piece in src/ui/hud.tscn was set to mouse_filter = 2 (IGNORE). A regression test was added (tests/manual/verify_look.gd, test_look_nothing_on_the_hud_swallows_the_mouse) that sends real motion events from the cursor position and fails if any HUD control stops them. From then on I held every new HUD element to the same rule, including the later HUD restyle and the MCP-built HEADSHOT label.

Follow-up prompt 2, the evidence (mine, word for word, after I played it on the MacBook):

"Its good, the mouse finally works, tho there is a problem, i would prefer that the zombies always run at me …"

So it's confirmed on real hardware, and the regression test passes in the project's check script (tools/check.sh, exit 0). The appendix has a second correction.

Appendix B — A second correction: zombies standing still

My prompt (word for word): "i would prefer that the zombies always run at me, instead of just staying there in there places"

First result (wrong): the assistant made every zombie keep "catching my scent" so it always heads for me. It then measured a real round (Endless, 20 zombies, me standing still) and found it hadn't worked. No zombie was unaware any more, but 13 of 20 still stood still while "investigating" 15–23 m away, and the average distance was stuck at 15.0 m after a minute.

Cause: my labyrinth's navigation mesh sits up to 0.7 m above the floor in places, and Godot's NavigationAgent3D counts a waypoint as reached in 3D using path_desired_distance = 0.6 m. So the first waypoint was the zombie's own spot lifted 0.7 m. It was never "reached", and the zombie kept steering at a point right above its head at zero speed.

Fix: Zombie._next_waypoint() in src/gameplay/zombie/zombie.gd measures waypoints flat and skips to the first path point that's actually ahead. Evidence: the same measurement afterwards had all 20 zombies within 6 m by 50 s, average distance 1.4 m.
